AI Friday, New Orleans. Sprint run September 2, 2026.

Four homepage directions, one afternoon, one model

This isn't a redesign of aifri.day. It's a test run: could Claude Fable 5.1, pointed at the AI Friday homepage with the method from Anshu Chimala's How to turn your AI into a world-class designer, come back with directions worth arguing about? Four Claude Code sessions ran in parallel, each with the same brief and a different direction. This page shows how the four came to be, the brief and prompts they got, and what they built.

How we got to four

Everything here happened in one claude.ai conversation with Fable 5.1 before any code was written. The full transcript is in the repo; this is the short version.

  1. Start with a feeling, not a mission

    Andrew opened with the article attached and a three-step sketch: come up with bold design languages, choose one, craft a prompt for another agent to build it, then run a creative feedback loop. Claude agreed with the arc and added a step zero.

    "Help people build more with AI in New Orleans" is a mission, not yet a feeling. Is it "you're welcome here even if you've never written code," or "there's a real scene here and you're missing it," or "this is where serious builders in the city hang out"? Those lead to very different visual worlds.

    Claude

    It also named the trap early: New Orleans is the biggest design asset and the easiest way to look like a tourist board.

  2. Name the audience and the one action

    Three questions. Who is the homepage for? What should a first-time visitor do? Is anything fixed?

    That's the problem, I think all of the above! For now, we want to invite AI curious or interested people. But our initial goal is getting as many people as possible to get started building but also for people using it to get tips and tricks. It is NOT targeted at a technical audience but generally speaking more people are in knowledge work/use a computer all day.

    Andrew

    The action: "Not sure yet." Fixed constraints: none. From that, Claude proposed the feeling that became the brief's spine, this is for you, and it's happening here, and picked RSVP to the next meetup as the primary action, because a Friday, a room, and a date are concrete in a way a Slack invite isn't. It also ruled out the default every AI-community site drifts toward: dark mode, monospace, terminal.

  3. Fifteen sparks, deliberately shallow

    The article's Discover stage says go broad before going deep, and let your own reactions do the choosing. Claude listed fifteen design languages in one breath. The three Andrew picked are marked.

    • Bulletin board
    • Shotgun house
    • Front porch
    • Hand-painted shop sign
    • Lab notebook
    • Recipe card
    • Community radio
    • Almanac
    • Public library
    • Transit map
    • Zine
    • Community garden
    • Sub-tropical light
    • Museum wall labels
    • Playground

    Ones I love: Shotgun house. This is super interesting! Front porch. Very familier new orleans scene. Think Mardi Gras and Jazz Fest. Hanging outside with a drink. Hand painted shop sign. Again, very new orleans.

    Andrew

  4. Three picks turn out to be three layers

    You didn't pick three competing looks, you picked three layers. Shotgun house is a structure, front porch is a scene, and shop sign is a type system. They could be three separate sites, or one site. That makes the parallel-sessions idea even better: run each alone, plus a fourth that combines them, and see which one surprises you.

    Claude

    Claude sharpened each pick into a direction with its own trap named: a 3D dollhouse, stock Southern hospitality, a Western saloon. Then it asked three questions that would decide the prompts.

    Does the shotgun house have to be read literally, or is it enough that locals feel it?
    "I'm not sure :)" Claude chose literal, on the grounds that with four sessions running, the bold version is the one worth seeing.
    Is the porch warm and quiet, or warm and lively?
    "Warm and lively."
    Does the whole page get lettered, or just headlines and one big sign?
    "I think the whole page lettered will be distracting." Headlines only.

    Perhaps we do four prompts/version. One of each of these and then one trying to combine. I will build with Claude Code. Again, make sure you encapsulate guidance from the article.

    Andrew

  5. One brief, four prompts, a critic, and a polish list

    Claude wrote a shared brief (the CLAUDE.md every session reads first), one direction prompt per session, a critic-loop prompt, and a polish checklist for a human to run last. It kept a table of where each of the article's techniques landed.

    Technique from the articleWhere it landed
    Seed strings for varietyEvery direction prompt, step 1
    Ambitious, specific promptsEvery direction prompt
    Critic subagent feedback loopCritic loop
    Image generationSetup plus every direction prompt
    Video generationSkipped for the proof of concept; noted for the polish pass
    Cut what doesn't add valuePolish pass
    Remove AI tellsCritic loop plus polish pass
    Rewrite copy by handShared brief plus polish pass

    Three calls Claude made on its own and flagged: the shotgun house goes literal; the tourist-trap list (beads, fleur-de-lis, jazz iconography, purple, green and gold, the French Quarter) is hard-banned in the brief; the critic loop stops after two iterations so no agent burns tokens chasing a score. Then it packaged the whole thing as a folder tree with a launcher, and Andrew ran it: four Claude Code sessions in four tmux windows, each on Fable 5.1, each generating its imagery through the OpenAI Codex CLI. Three of the windows died on launch when four instances started at the same instant. They were relaunched by hand.

The brief

Every session got the same CLAUDE.md before its direction prompt. Placeholder meetup facts, invented member projects, a hard list of what New Orleans is not allowed to look like, and an instruction to stop and say so rather than fake textures in CSS if image generation wasn't available.

The four directions

Each session read the brief, got one direction prompt, generated a random string to settle every decision the prompt left open, wrote a design note before touching code, generated its imagery, built a single static page, checked it frame by frame in a browser, and wrote notes. None has been through the critic loop yet. The copy on every page is scaffolding a person will rewrite.

{{exhibits}}

What happens next

The critic loop runs in each session: a fresh Fable 5.1 subagent sees only screenshots, names the aesthetic, imagines how a top studio would execute it for a neighborhood meetup, scores it out of ten, and the session revises. Two rounds, then it stops. Andrew's gut reactions go back to the sprint conversation for a sharpening round. Whatever survives gets the polish pass by hand: cut a third of it, hunt the tells, rewrite every line. Nothing here goes live until then.
