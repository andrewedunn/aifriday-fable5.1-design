# NOTES

I built the page as one walk: porch, four rooms, back step, with a photographed doorway between each pair and a single hairline "shot line" running the whole height at the point every door lines up on. All thirteen shipped surfaces are generated photographs (Codex CLI, billed to the ChatGPT subscription) — the porch at golden hour, four beadboard walls under four different lights, four views of the same doorway, the back steps at blue hour, and two blank hand-painted boards left empty so the copy stays live type. A generated floor didn't earn a place and isn't shipped; its source is still in `assets/_src/`. No texture, light, or surface anywhere is CSS. Type is Libre Franklin 800/900 for the signs (Franklin Gothic is the sign painter's alphabet, and it is neither saloon nor farmhouse nor café) and Karla for everything else, both self-hosted.

**What fought with what.** Mostly the house fought the porch, and the house won too many rounds before I cut it back. My first pass gave each doorway a near-full-screen band, which put 48% of the scroll inside hallways — the house doing mood and pacing instead of just sequence. I couldn't shorten the bands without cropping the lintel off and turning a doorway into a dark slot, so I rebuilt the door images with mirrored wall extending off both sides (checked pixel-continuous at all four joins after my first attempt got the mirror order backwards and left a visible seam). Now you see the door from across the room, which is the actual shotgun view, and the doorways are down to about a third of the page. The porch fought back on mobile: the painted board sat exactly on top of the people and the empty chair, deleting the entire argument, so on narrow screens the board moves to the top of the screen and the ask to the bottom with the porch left visible between them. The signs were the easiest to keep honest — they never left the headers — but four instances of one plank photo read as four copies of one photo, so two of them hang mirrored. The light progression also fought legibility twice, and both times the photograph was right and my layout was wrong: cream copy in the back room landed on the lamp's amber pool, and the phone crop put beadboard grooves at the same scale as the letter strokes. I mirrored the dim wall so the lamp falls behind the sign instead, and aimed each mobile wall crop at its lit side. I audited contrast by hiding the text, screenshotting the real rendered backgrounds viewport by viewport, and scoring the 5th-percentile pixel under every text box; that cleared all failures, and the remaining warnings sit at median 6–7:1 with only the darkest grooves dipping.

**What I'd do next.** The four middle rooms are content-light next to how much wall they show — I'd either write more into them or tighten the vertical rhythm, and I suspect the answer is more copy, since the owner is rewriting all of this anyway. The kitchen is supposed to be the room you stop in and it currently uses the same two-column layout as everywhere else; giving it one deliberate break in the pattern would earn the Slack CTA more. I'd also test the walk on a real phone rather than an emulated viewport, since `background-attachment: fixed` is the one thing here likely to feel different on device. Sources for every generated image are kept in `assets/_src/` as the original PNGs; `assets/*.webp` is what the page loads (~1.5M total).
